# Module 2 quiz — Error analysis

This quiz checks the four things Module 2 exists to install: that a trace tells you what the pipeline
did and not what the answer said, that a note written before a label is worth more than a label, that a
counted taxonomy still has to be argued about before it becomes a plan, and that a batch of traces is a
sample of the filter that produced it.

Every question is a situation you will meet in your own work, not a definition. Four options, one
correct. **Pass mark: 70%** — seven of the nine questions. You can retake it; the questions do not
change, so a second attempt is worth taking only after you have re-read the lesson behind the one you
got wrong.

What it covers, in the order the lessons build it:

- [02.1 · Reading a trace](../lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md) — the span tree, and the failure classes
  that live in the pipeline rather than in the answer text.
- [02.2 · Open coding: writing notes on failures](../lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md) — observation before
  label, and what a taxonomy with one row is really telling you.
- [02.3 · Axial coding: building a failure taxonomy](../lessons/module-02/lesson-03.md) — naming the rows,
  counting them, and ranking on frequency, severity and cost to fix.
- [02.4 · Sampling: which traces to read, and when to stop](../lessons/module-02/lesson-04.md) — random,
  error-filtered and complaint-filtered batches, and coverage instead of a quota of 30.

Two reminders before you start. First, the labs use a **seeded educational model** for traces and
verdicts: every statistic is computed for real in your browser, and no number in this course is a
measurement of a named model or system in the wild. Second, more than half of these questions are about
which batch you would read and what you would do with it, because that decision is what makes an error
analysis worth the afternoon it costs.
