# Module 1 quiz — Why evals

This quiz checks the four things Module 1 exists to install: that a pass rate without an interval is not
evidence, that "good" has to be written down before it can be labelled, that improvement happens one
change at a time, and that you know what the labs in this course are and are not.

Every question is a situation you will meet in your own work, not a definition. Four options, one correct.
**Pass mark: 70%** — seven of the nine questions. You can retake it; the questions do not change, so a
second attempt is worth taking only after you have re-read the lesson behind the one you got wrong.

What it covers, in the order the lessons build it:

- [01.1 · Vibes versus evals](lessons/module-01/lesson-01.md) — the three numbers, and why the interval
  is the one that decides whether a change is real.
- [01.2 · What "good" means for one feature](lessons/module-01/lesson-02.md) — decidable criteria, the
  citation clause, and why a vague check on a good criterion still produces confident nonsense.
- [01.3 · The loop: analyse, measure, improve](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md) — pinning a baseline, the
  noise floor, and the cost of changing three things at once.
- [01.4 · A tour of the Eval Lab](lessons/module-01/lesson-04.md) — the labs, the device check, and the
  honesty rule about modelled judge verdicts.

Two reminders before you start. First, the labs use a **seeded educational model** for judge verdicts:
every statistic is computed for real in your browser, and no number in this course is a measurement of a
named model in the wild. Second, most of these questions are about what you would *do* next, not about
what a number is, because that is the part that decides whether an eval helps your team.
